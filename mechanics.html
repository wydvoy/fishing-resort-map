Overview

Result

31 tables. 29 have identical numbers to the Japanese disc; text differs only by language. Lure data, fish data, spawn tables, spots, rods, reels, baits, bait affinity, size bands, titles, activities, board targets, quests, trolling routes and hit tables are all the same.

Real version differences (Pacar Beach terrain only):

  • fishing_points.csv: the platform at x -64.37 is 37.87 m deep instead of 43.20 m (centre z -106.18 instead of -107.40).
  • spot_depth.csv: spots 6, 9, 16, 18, 19, 20 and 25 have less land under them on the USA disc; spot 20 median depth 5.16 m (JP 5.17), spot 27 4.49 m (JP 4.41).

What is verified and what is not

  • Verified on the USA disc: every table value above and every number in the tables, by regenerating them from our disc and diffing against theirs. For lure colour, the code paths too (callers, mode 0, action bonus function).
  • Taken from their code reading, not re-read in USA code: behaviour described in prose, such as distances (60 m, 80 m, 30 m), timers, the 320-fish limit, interest thresholds (15, 40, 8, 30), stamina and landing rules, and the radar and cloud-shape rules. The data those rules read is identical, so they very likely hold, but the USA code constants were not each checked. Items they marked unconfirmed are marked the same here.
  • Nothing here was tested live in game.

Unused data

  • Fish 26 "Koi" (Japanese name "unused id (Nishikigoi)") exists in the data but spawns nowhere. Forced in with an external tool it shows no shadow and is recorded as a Horse Mackerel.
  • TestStage is a sealed-off area with spawn tables but no water, so nothing spawns. Forced in, it is a blue and white checkerboard; the rod works but the float falls on the ground.
  • Trolling-only fish have full normal fish parameters and can be caught if spawned. They spawned and caught Mahi-mahi (easy) and Dunkleosteus (as strong as Hyneria, with matching parameters). On the result screen the model's angle breaks and the tail points diagonally up.
  • Wholeisland (the open-sea map) has its own spawn table besides the Tropical, Temperate and Frigid ones, but it is never read. It mixes freshwater, saltwater and trolling fish (Pond Smelt, Catfish, Great White Shark and more) and has per-time-of-day size factors (White Sturgeon 0.98, 0.94 by day, and so on). Everywhere else the size factor is 1.0 at all times.
  • The colour function has three unused multiplier tables (lure colour); this one is our finding, not theirs.

01
Spawn tables, time of day, copy spawns, refill and shadows

Spawning

Reading the odds

Each fishing area's water is split into rectangles called spots, and each spot has its own spawn table. Every time a fish spawns, its species is drawn from that spot's table. The chance is weight ÷ the table's total weight; a weight-2 fish is picked exactly twice as often as a weight-1 fish. Spot size plays no part.

spawn_rates.csv has three columns of odds: pct_early (no quest fish and no stocked fish yet), pct_standard (quest fish unlocked) and pct_all_unlocked (stock passwords entered too).

Time of day

Spawn tables carry morning, day, evening and night columns, but in every table that is used all four are equal: time of day never changes which species spawn or how often.

What time does change is the cap on how many fish a spot can hold at once. 205 of 468 spots change their cap; 204 of those peak in the morning and 203 are lowest in the day. Example: Panas Jungle spot 9 holds 20 in the morning, 12 by day, 14 in the evening, 16 at night. For these spots the morning cap is 1.33x the day cap (median), up to 2.5x.

Band Game clock
Morning 6:00 to 11:59
Day 12:00 to 15:59
Evening 16:00 to 17:59
Night 18:00 to 5:59

When the odds lie

  • Shallow water. A fish never spawns where the water is shallower than a quarter of its length. The check happens after the species is drawn, so shallow spots get fewer big fish than the table says. The effect is small: of 4,012 spot and fish pairs, 11 move by 1 point or more, at most 3.8 points. Depth per spot is in spot_depth.csv.
  • Copy spawns pick from fish already nearby, so you can hook a species the spot's table doesn't list (below).

Normal spawns

  1. A spot spawns nothing unless the player is within 60 m of its rectangle.
  2. On entering that range, two thirds of the cap spawn at once.
  3. The rest trickle in, one fish per frame across all spots together.

One spawn:

  1. Draw the species from the spot table. If it lands on a locked fish, redraw up to 17 times.
  2. Decide the length.
  3. Pick a random point in the rectangle.
  4. Set the depth to 20% of the water depth.
  5. Cancel if the point is out of water, shallower than a quarter of the fish's length (0.5 m for a 2 m fish), more than 80 m from the player, or, while waiting for a bite, more than 30 m away inside the 120° cone behind the player.
  6. Create the fish and roll whether it is invisible (shadows).

Spot data also lists depth weights (20%, 50%, 80%, random 20 to 80%), but the way the draw is written always picks the first, so fish only ever spawn at 20% depth. Leaving range and coming back refills two thirds of the cap again.

Refill speed

Below the cap a spot refills continuously. Once one of its fish starts pecking a float, refills slow down for 60 seconds: one fish per refill_sec (in spots.csv). Another fish starting to peck resets the 60 seconds.

When a spot is full, the oldest fish fades out every 2x refill_sec and is replaced at once.

Typical refill_sec: Teman Paradise Beach 6 s, Panas Jungle 42 s, Dua Ribu Lake and Tidur River 36 s, Safir Cave 120 s. At most 320 fish exist at once.

Copy spawns

If nothing is biting 18 to 22 seconds after the float settles, one fish spawns at the float (unconfirmed). No more spawn until you recast (unconfirmed).

The species is picked from fish within 80 m of the player, excluding grade 3 and above, LL-size fish and locked fish. Each species has the same chance however many of it are around. With no candidates it takes the lowest-grade, smallest fish nearby; with no fish around it is a Horse Mackerel.

These fish are always invisible, get no bait spot adjust and don't count toward any spot's cap. They swim around normally before biting. This is why you can catch species a spot's table doesn't list.

Piranha in Emerald Marsh. Piranha, Pacu and African Pike caught in Emerald Marsh come from copy spawns. The winding river north-east of the marsh passes within about 60 m of it (call it point A). Point A is within 80 m of the marsh's landing and stays within 80 m on the way in, so its fish never despawn. Shaking off shadows in the marsh keeps spawning random nearby fish at the float, and point A's Piranha, Pacu or Average Driftwood can be chosen. The landing is over 90 m from the marsh's east bank, so it doesn't work the other way.

Open sea

Fish spawn within ±30 m of the cruiser, 3 to 5 m below it, by the cruiser's position. Fish more than 30 m from the cruiser vanish. Open-sea fish are always invisible, which is why you see nothing from the deck even though fish are there. Some of them show on the radar (trolling).

The result pair

When a fish is landed it disappears and two fish of the same species are made for the result scene. One copies the catch's length, rank and experience and is the one you see; the fish you fought and the one flapping on screen are different objects. The other is a base-size fish you never see.

The landing animation is built for the base size, so the base fish runs it to measure body position and the visible fish is placed to match. Its drawn size shrinks the gap to base size by its hold style:

Hold (fish.csv hold) Share of the size gap shown
holds the line 100%
palm 50%
two hands 50% (Largehead Hairtail 25%)
underwater 20%

A two-hand King Salmon with base 100 cm caught at 120 cm looks 110 cm.

Jumping. A fish jumps when it is under 100 cm, its shadow shows, it is over 15 m away and fewer than 2 fish are jumping. Each fish rolls about once a second at 10%. After a jump it needs 25 to 33 more successful rolls before jumping again. A jumping fish shows from any distance, and jumping resets its stored interest.

02
Interest, steps to a bite, reach, peck time and giving up

Bites

The steps

Every fish has a value for how drawn it is to your rig; this page calls it interest. With a float, once the float's appeal reaches a fish it goes through:

  1. Build interest. It keeps swimming around where it spawned while interest builds. At 15 it becomes a candidate for that float.
  2. Wait. Interest stays put.
  3. Head for the float. It swims to just below the float. Interest keeps building and moves on at 40. With a reel that suits the fish, 40 comes almost at once.
  4. Wait to peck. It keeps swimming and stops when it arrives. After a set time it moves on.
  5. Peck. If it is under the float, its mouth touches the rig and the float dips.

Steps 4 and 5 start whether or not the fish has arrived (giving up), so a fish can finish step 5 without touching the float and swim off.

With a lure, a fish heads in as soon as it is a candidate and pecks as soon as interest hits 40.

Each fish has a lure accept rate (lure colour). With lures it is rolled when the fish becomes a candidate; a miss resets interest to 0.

The times for step 2, step 4 and step 5 come from the fish's grade:

Grade Step 2 (s) Step 4 (s) Step 5 peck time (s)
0 1-4 2-5 5-15
1 1-5 2-6 5-15
2 2-6 3-7 4.5-13.5
3 2-7 3-8 3.75-11.25
4 3-8 4-9 2.75-8.25
5 4-9 5-10 2-6

Steps 2 and 4 pick a whole number of seconds evenly from the range. Peck time here excludes the rank and bait parts (peck time).

Interest

Interest runs 0 to 100 and only builds in steps 1 and 3. Each frame the rig's appeal reaches the fish, it adds the product of:

  • reel factor (tackle)
  • bait affinity factor (float, step 1 only) (bait)
  • lure colour factor (lure) (lure colour)
  • action match, x1.2 (lure)
  • x2 for fish with accept rate 1.0
  • facing factor (lure) (lure reach)

In step 5 interest rises 1 per frame while the appeal reaches the fish. Out of reach it falls 0.15 per frame in step 3, and 0.1 per frame in step 5 unless the mouth is on the rig. A still float keeps reaching a fish that is heading for it, so interest only really drops with lures that move away.

The game's own tutorial does say fish peck because they are "interested".

Reach

How far the rig's appeal reaches. A fish that leaves the reach on its way keeps coming, but its interest drains.

Bait. Horizontal distance between float and fish. Reach starts at 0.05 m after the cast, grows at the bait's rate up to its cap while the float is still (bait), and drops back to 0.05 m if the float moves for more than 0.2 s (reeling, for example).

Lure reach

Straight-line distance including depth. Reach is the lure's base value plus a bonus from how you work it: the more you vary the speed, the wider it gets.

A lure whose action type matches the fish's reaches it from any direction. Otherwise it depends on where the lure is along the fish's length:

Lure position Result
More than 0.71 m behind the fish no appeal
From 0.71 m behind to 0.5 m in front interest x1.2
More than 0.5 m in front interest x1

Several candidates

Of the candidates, the one nearest the rig is chosen and may head for it. Once chosen it keeps that right even if another fish gets closer.

With a lure the chosen fish comes straight in and stays nearest, so no other fish comes until it leaves.

With a float the chosen fish loiters a while first. Meanwhile another fish can become nearest and be chosen too, and then both head for the float. Loitering lasts longer at higher grade, and every ancient fish is grade 5, so junk and common fish often join them.

With several fish under the float, the hook takes whichever fish's mouth touched first; if it lets go, the next one to touch takes its place.

Peck time

Peck time = random 5 to 15 s × grade factor × rank factor + a Free Bait bonus.

Grade factors are in size and rank. Rank factors:

Rank S A B C D E
Factor 0.85 0.9 0.95 1 1 1

With a float, a bonus is added from the fish's Free Bait affinity, whatever bait you use, after the spawn spot's bait adjust:

Adjusted affinity 0 1 2 3 4 5
Seconds added 0 0 0 2 4 8
Free Bait affinity adjust +1 adjust 0 adjust -1, -2
0 0 0 0
1 0 0 0
2 2 0 0
3 4 2 0
4 8 4 2
5 8 8 4

Copy-spawned fish get no spot adjust, so they use the adjust-0 column. L and LL fish have no Free Bait value and get nothing.

Peck time starts counting when step 5 begins and keeps counting whether or not the mouth is on the rig, including while the fish is still swimming in or another fish is pecking. Real contact time is shorter.

Giving up

A candidate gives up when the float moves, peck time runs out, interest falls under 8 in step 3, or interest falls under 30 in step 5. A still float keeps interest up, so interest give-ups are mostly a lure thing. A full spot also replaces its oldest fish, which can remove a fish that is heading in or pecking (refill).

A fish can go through the pecking motion and leave without dipping the float even if you never moved it: peck time ran out before its mouth reached the rig. The mouth counts as on the rig within 0.2 m horizontally of the float. A fish swims fast until 20% of its starting distance is left, then slows so the gap shrinks only about 20% per second, while steps 4 and 5 run on interest and time alone. Closing that last 20% to 0.2 m takes:

Starting distance 3 m 6 m 10 m 14 m
Time ~4.9 s ~8.0 s ~10.2 s ~11.7 s

High-grade fish have short peck times and wide-reach bait pulls fish from further away, so rare fish like ancient fish most often come close and leave without pecking. It looks like they peck slightly off the float.

Lure colour

How a lure's colours change how fast a fish gets interested, read from the USA disc (SFVE). Tables: download.

The model and most table locations come from the Japanese research (built from the Japanese disc S22JAF). Every value was re-read from the USA files and main.dol. How a fish picks its favourite

Each fish species has a base colour (fishData.bin word 21). When a fish spawns, its favourite is the base plus a random 0 to 6, never minus, wrapping past 12. The odds depend on the weather (pointer table 0x8052178C, four identical time-of-day rows each; read by 0x8005EE78). The draw is int((total+1) * rand), so the first option gets one extra ticket.

Colour multiplier

Function 0x80134734: take the ring distance from each lure colour to the favourite (0 to 6), keep the smaller, add 1 for a two-colour lure, cap at 6, and look up 0x804C2A94.

Distance 0 1 2 3 4 5 6
Two-colour lure 0.8 0.6 0.4 0.2 0 0 0
One-colour lure 1.0 0.8 0.6 0.4 0.2 0 0

At 0 the fish gains no interest at all. While another fish is pecking the same lure, the spot's bait adjust (table 0x804C2A60, -6..+6) is subtracted from the distance.

The function can select three more 7-value tables (0x804C2AB0, 0x804C2ACC, 0x804C2AE8), but both callers (0x8011D2B8, 0x8011DC90) pass mode 0, so they are unused. The Japanese research does not mention them.

The length used is the smaller of the fish's length and its species base size, so it is almost fixed per species: a base-200 cm Tarpon at 250 cm is still 1x on HF or X.

X rods say "50 cm+", but the check uses a 200 cm ceiling, so species with base over 200 cm get 8x on X exactly like HF. In normal fishing those are King of Herrings, Pirarucu, Alligator Gar, Platinum Alligator Gar, Electric Eel, White Sturgeon, Goblin Shark and Hyneria.

So Berserker equals Hamstring and Titan equals Storm Killer in gauge movement and cast multiplier. Rod length is the same for all rods. The only differences: X rods accept LL lures, and on fish under 150 cm an X rod bends less (an HF rod starts bending above 50% gauge on a 50-150 cm fish, an X rod above 75%). Bending is visual only.

Their check: they measured gauge speed by reading memory while fighting Alligator Gar with Titan + Infinity and Storm Killer + Lunker Keeper, and fought Hyneria with both. Gauge speed was identical and Hyneria was catchable with Storm Killer + Lunker Keeper with no felt difference. Fish under 150 cm were not measured, but rod bend is not part of the gauge maths.

The trolling rod has gauge max 1000 and Journey-reel factors.

Tension gauge

Reeling raises the gauge, not reeling lowers it. Per second:

Situation Change per second
Reeling at 50% or above + speed A × reel strength × reel-hold factor × miss factor × reel value × rod over-size
Reeling below 50% + speed B × match factor × reel value
Not reeling at 50% or above − speed B × match factor × reel value
Not reeling below 50% − speed A × miss factor × reel value × rod over-size

Reel-hold factor goes from 0 to 1 over 2 s of continuous reeling. Miss and match factors come from rod tilt (below).

Gauge Effect Time

Durability starts at 100 and never recovers. Slack margin starts at up to 1 depending on the hook rating (Excellent to Fish On) and never recovers.

The fight also fails if the fish gets too far away front-to-back. The limit is set at the hook: fish hooked far away can go further. The on-screen distance is front-to-back only.

Bringing the fish in

How fast a reeled fish approaches scales with the gauge (faster closer to 50%, maximum from 50% up) and with size (smaller is faster; fish 200 cm and over barely come in until their stamina drops).

Landing. While stamina is above 40%, the fish must be near the surface: within 0.75x its body height. At 40% or below, staying under 2 m from the rod tip for 8 s also lands it.

Stamina starts at 100% and bottoms out at 10%. It drains 1% ÷ stamina value per second; reeling adds 3% × reel strength ÷ stamina value. Within 0.5 m of the landing point it drains 10x as fast. Arowana drains at 0.75x and Peacock Bass at 1.5x; why only these two is unknown.

Highest stamina values: Xiphactinus 4, Helicoprion 4, Hikarimuji Koi 4, Dunkleosteus 3.5, Hyneria 3.5, Swordfish 3, Giant Squid 3, Striped Marlin 3, Black Marlin 3, Halibut 3, N. Pacific Giant Octopus 3, Indo-Pacific Blue Marlin 3.

First run

A hooked fish swims away from the rod, angled 10 to 45° to one side, switching sides now and then. A fish hooked closer to the rod tip than its grade's run distance (grade) first swims straight away for that distance ±1 m, at most 4 s (unconfirmed).

Top speed depends on length, size relative to base, and a per-species factor; bigger fish of the same species are faster.

After landing

Grade 4+ fish and LL fish always get the special jingle. Others get it at S rank or better (exactly: a length the encyclopedia records as S).

07
How trolling hits work, lures, routes and radar

Trolling

How a fish gets hooked

While trolling, each rod repeats on its own:

  1. Wait 5 to 10 s (7.5 s on average).
  2. Pick one species from the current route's table.
  3. Roll a hit with the chance for your lure and that species' base colour. On a miss, go back to 1.
  4. On a hit the rod bends; pick that rod within 2 to 5 s to start the fight.

The species is chosen first, so the lure changes both how often you get hits and which fish they are. Normal lure colour and size checks are not used, and weather doesn't matter. For the first 3 minutes after the rods go out, the hit chance is multiplied by elapsed time ÷ 3 minutes. Fish swimming in the sea play no part.

Lure versus base colour

Lures are numbered left to right on the setup screen. Percent; 30% and up in bold (USA table 0x804F8290).

Every pair is at least 5%, so any lure can catch every fish on a route. Base colours: fish.csv base_color.

Routes

A route is a loop of waypoints at sea. Starting to troll picks the route with the waypoint nearest the cruiser; the cruiser joins at that waypoint and then loops the route. Route numbers are the Japanese research's labels. images/trolling/routes.png draws them, and start-zones.png colours the sea by which route you get when starting there.

A route's fish never change with time or weather; the only change is that stocking adds Sailfish.

Per route and lure, trolling_route_summary.csv has the hit chance per check (after the 3-minute ramp) and the average wait. Average wait for one rod = 7.5 s ÷ chance: 50% means 15 s, 5% means 150 s; five rods divide that by 5. trolling_route_fish.csv breaks it down per species (pick_rate, hit_rate, per_check, share_of_hits, with and without the stock password).

Radar

The open-sea radar shows real fish dots and fake ones. They look the same, but fake dots move fast.
